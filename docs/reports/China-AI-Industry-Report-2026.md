# China AI Industry Report - 2026 Edition

## Foreword

The numbers are staggering. China's daily token usage exceeded 140 trillion in March 2026, up more than a thousandfold from 100 billion at the start of 2024-11. The AI core industry had already surpassed RMB 1.2 trillion by the end of 2025, with over 6,000 AI enterprises operating in the country-1-5.
But 2026 is not a year of simple growth — it is a year of fundamental transformation. The shift from "digital economy" to "intelligent economy" is underway, and China has become the proving ground for a new paradigm where AI moves beyond information processing into physical execution, decision-making, and full-stack integration across industries.
This is not a superficial summary. This is the strategic analysis that covers: the true state of China's AI competitiveness against US models, the economics of token consumption and cloud infrastructure build-out, the emerging "intelligent economy" thesis and what it means for business, the investment community's debate on China AI in 2026, the pricing war dynamics and who is winning, the regulatory landscape with AI legislation under active development, and the commercialization trajectory that investors are watching.-2-3-10

### 1.2 The "Intelligent Economy" Thesis

The core analytical framework for understanding China's AI trajectory in 2026 comes from the China New Generation AI Technology Industry Development Report 2026, published by Nankai University's Institute of New Generation AI Strategic Development in May 2026-1-5-9.
Key concept: The AI economy is fundamentally different from the digital economy. The digital economy was driven by data itself. The intelligent economy is driven by "intelligent factors" produced by AI systems — the fusion of data, algorithms, and computing power into actionable intelligence-5-9.
The report's lead researcher, Professor Liu Gang, describes this as: "The AI system continuously produces 'intelligence,' which combines with human wisdom to form 'hybrid intelligence.' This hybrid intelligence breaks through the limitations of human decision-making, resource allocation, and scientific research, creating the foundation for a new productivity leap."-5-9

### 1.3 China's AI Turning Point: The Token Consumption Benchmark

2026 has been widely characterized as the turning point year for AI development in China. The evidence is in token consumption data-1-5:
February 2026: China's weekly token consumption on the OpenRouter platform surpassed the United States for the first time-1
May 4-10, 2026: Chinese token consumption reached 2.11 times that of the United States-1
This shift signals that the "intelligent economy" as a distinct economic form is first emerging in China. The scale of AI usage — not just development — is now unmatched globally.

### 3.3 The Pricing War — A Structural Reality

Between March and June 2026, US API prices rose an average of 89%, while Chinese prices remained flat or declined-6. June alone saw a 2% price drop, driven primarily by MiniMax's 33% price reduction-6.
Why the divergence:
US: Storage chip costs and hardware costs are rising-6
China: Intense domestic competition prevents price increases; the market is saturated with models-6
Jefferies analysts' concern: "Compared to model capabilities, the price war in Chinese AI models is our current worry."-6 The concern is not capability gap but sustainable returns. With rising domestic chip costs and persistent storage costs, pricing power remains constrained.
DeepSeek recently raised V4 Flash and Pro model prices by up to fourfold-8. Even after the increase, V4 Pro's blended price remains below GLM-5.2, indicating that price discipline has not fully returned to the market.

## Part IV: Competitive Dynamics — The Five Key Debates

Goldman Sachs has framed the China AI narrative around five core investment debates-11:
Debate 1: China vs. US Model Performance
Goldman's take: China models are competitive in pricing, inference speed, and agent task completion rates. The gap with US frontier models (GPT/Opus/Gemini) has narrowed across multiple dimensions-11.
The constraints: Tight compute constraints are pushing Chinese AI companies along a unique development path — focusing on training and inference efficiency, data quality, and post-training optimization, rather than brute-force scaling-11.
Debate 2: Fragmented Competition — The Differentiation Challenge
The problem: Entry barriers are falling. A training cycle of less than three months (Tencent Hy3.0) demonstrates how quickly new entrants can emerge-11.
The challenge for investors: How does any company sustain a moat?
200 billion to 300 billion parameter models (the agent application sweet spot) are now crowded-11
Independent AI companies have advantage in organizational efficiency and decision speed-11
Internet giants have cash flow advantage for infrastructure investment but must create independent incentives for AI teams-11
The differentiation imperative: Developers and businesses are not just choosing models — they are choosing ecosystems. The winning players will need to offer more than just technical performance.
Debate 3: Token Growth Sustainability
Goldman's take: The token growth trend is sustainable. Cloud providers have pricing upside potential-11.
The data driver: China's daily token usage is growing at a pace that supports continued cloud infrastructure investment-11. The total token demand is now large enough that even with domestic price competition, volume growth can sustain revenue expansion.
Debate 4: AI Native vs. Internet Giants
The structural tension: Independent AI model companies (DeepSeek, Zhipu, MiniMax) must compete with internet giants (Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu) that have deep pockets and captive cloud ecosystems-11.
Goldman's view: Independent companies have faster decision-making and more focused organizational structures. Internet giants have the capital to build infrastructure but face internal friction in attracting AI talent-11.
Debate 5: The Consumer AI Agent Trajectory
Goldman's take: The consumer AI agent market is likely to follow the same pattern as the cloud market — consolidation, with the strongest players capturing disproportionate share-11.
The key differentiator: Programming ability, multi-modal capability, and task completion rates will be the core drivers of pricing power. The future may shift from per-token charging to per-task-completion pricing-11.

## Part V: Application and Industry Penetration

### 5.1 The "AI+" Policy Framework

The National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) has issued "AI+" policy documents covering more than ten industries, including manufacturing, healthcare, energy, and transportation-3-7.
The "AI+" strategy structure:
Policy layer: Industry-specific implementation documents
Infrastructure layer: National AI application pilot bases in manufacturing, healthcare, and transportation-3
Deployment layer: State-owned enterprises encouraged to open high-value application scenarios-3-7
Recent announcement: The NDRC is preparing additional supporting documents to accelerate "AI+" implementation, with continued push for SOEs to open application scenarios and create benchmark AI applications-3.

### 5.2 The Leading Application: Autonomous Driving

AI Report 2026 finding: Autonomous driving is the most industrialized AI application area-5-9.
The spillover effect: Technologies and experience accumulated through vehicle-road-cloud coordination in autonomous driving are migrating to humanoid robots, low-altitude aircraft, and industrial automation-5-9.

### 5.3 Manufacturing and Smart Healthcare

Smart manufacturing: Identified as a leading industry capable of driving industrial upgrading-5-9.
Smart healthcare: Shifting medical models from "treatment-centered" to "health-centered" — a fundamental transformation in how healthcare is delivered-5-9.
AI for Science: A new scientific research paradigm is emerging, where AI-driven research becomes the standard approach to discovery-5-9.

### 5.4 The Intelligent Agent Era

2026 is seeing the large-scale deployment of intelligent agents (智能体) and embodied AI (具身智能) moving from development to deployment-1-5. This shift — from information intelligence to physical intelligence — is the defining characteristic of the current AI expansion.

## Part VI: Regulatory and Policy Environment

### 6.1 AI Legislation — In Active Development

The NDRC is currently conducting AI legislative research, with the goal of strengthening safety governance capabilities and promoting the "beneficial, safe, and fair" development of AI-3-7-10.
What this means for business:
Legislation is coming — the question is when, not if
The framework is expected to be comprehensive rather than piecemeal, with the State Council's 2026 legislative work plan explicitly calling for comprehensive AI legislation-10
Principles being signaled: development, safety, and human-centered as a coordinated value structure-10

### 6.2 The "Classified and Graded" Governance Model

Proposed regulatory framework: A "classified and graded" governance model where risk is the primary determinant of regulatory intensity-10.
Key regulatory concepts:
Classification: Based on function, purpose, application scenario, and impact object
Grading: Based on likelihood of harm, severity of consequences, scope of impact, reversibility, and system autonomy-10
Implication: Higher-risk AI applications (healthcare, autonomous vehicles, critical infrastructure) will face stronger regulatory scrutiny than lower-risk applications.

### 6.3 Existing Policy Framework

Before comprehensive legislation, the NDRC has already issued:
AI Technology Ethics Review and Service Measures-7
AI Anthropomorphic Interaction Service Management Interim Measures-7
"AI+" policy documents covering more than ten industries-3

## Part VII: The "Intelligent Economy" Scenario — One Contrarian View

This is not a forecast — it's a scenario to track.
The consensus narrative treats China's AI trajectory as a steady ascent toward global leadership, with revenue growth inevitably following adoption growth.
There is an alternative interpretation: China's AI sector may be building revenue capacity faster than it is building a sustainable business model.
Why this matters:
Pricing pressure: The price war is structural, not cyclical. With multiple capable models competing for the same enterprise users, pricing power may remain constrained indefinitely
The valuation gap: Public market valuations of listed AI companies ($137B peak for Zhipu) may be disconnected from underlying profitability ($1B ARR on $80B market cap)-8
Cloud winners vs. model losers: The value capture dynamic may resemble the mobile internet era — the platforms (cloud providers) capture the value while the applications (models) remain commoditized
Signal to watch:
Pricing discipline: Can market leaders raise prices without losing share? The DeepSeek V4 price increase will be a key test-8
Enterprise willingness to pay: Are businesses willing to pay for premium models, or do they default to the lowest-cost option?
Consolidation timing: Which independent model companies are acquired or exit the market? The Goldman Sachs expectation is for consolidation-11

## Part VIII: Outlook and Key Signals

### 8.1 What to Watch in H2 2026 and Beyond

AI legislation finalization: The NDRC's legislative research will likely yield concrete proposals in 2027. How comprehensive and how restrictive will the legislation be?-3-7-10
Profitability turn: The 2026 "pain period" may ease in 2027 as scale improves margins. The key metric to watch: API margin improvement from 20-30% toward 30-40%-2
Model performance gap closure: GLM-5.3's performance relative to Fable 5 will signal whether the narrowing gap is sustainable-8
Cloud capital expenditure trajectory: Over 50% of cloud CAPEX is flowing to AI hardware. A slowdown would signal a peak in infrastructure build-out-2
Consumer AI agent adoption: The shift from enterprise to consumer AI will define the next growth phase. To date, the consumer market remains nascent-11

### 8.2 Risk Factors

Geopolitical risk: U.S.-Iran conflict escalation (beginning late February 2026) has affected IT budgets and CIO sentiment-4
Pricing pressure risk: Continued price wars may prevent return on AI investment
Hardware cost risk: Domestic chip costs are rising, squeezing margins-6
Regulatory uncertainty: China's first AI comprehensive legislation could introduce significant new compliance burdens
Demand cannibalization: AI spending is beginning to eat traditional software budgets — 22% of AI funding now comes from existing software budgets (up from 10%)-4
