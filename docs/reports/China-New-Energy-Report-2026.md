# China New Energy Report - 2026 Edition

## Foreword

China has built the world's largest and most comprehensive clean energy industry chain-1-4. The country provides 70% of global wind power equipment and 80% of solar PV modules-1, driving global wind and solar costs down by more than 60% and 80%, respectively-4. In 2024 alone, China's energy transition investment reached USD 818 billion — surpassing the combined investments of the United States, the United Kingdom, and the European Union-1.
But the scale of the numbers can obscure the strategic reality. This is not merely an industry — it is becoming the structural core of China's economy. In 2025, clean energy technologies drove more than a third of China's GDP growth and more than 90% of the net rise in investment-6. Without the clean energy sector, China's GDP would have expanded by 3.5% instead of the reported 5.0% — missing the target of "around 5%" growth by a wide margin-6-8.
This is not a superficial summary. This is the strategic analysis that covers: the true scale and composition of China's new energy economy, the "New Three" export phenomenon and its geopolitical implications, the structural shift from policy-driven to market-driven growth, the coming milestone — solar PV generation surpassing coal in 2026, the investment dynamics and the manufacturing rebalancing underway, the provincial distribution of clean energy manufacturing, and the outlook for H2 2026 and beyond.

## Part I: The Macro Picture — Scale and Economic Significance

### 1.1 The Clean Energy Economy — A New Global Power

China's clean energy economy has nearly doubled in value from RMB 8.4 trillion in 2022 to RMB 15.4 trillion in 2025-6-8. This is comparable to the entire GDP of Canada or Brazil — positioning China's clean energy industry as the eighth-largest economy in the world if considered as a standalone entity-6-8.

## Part II: The "New Three" — NEVs, Batteries, and Solar PV

### 2.1 New Energy Vehicles (NEVs)

2025 position: NEV production value reached RMB 3.94 trillion in 2025, the largest single contributor to clean energy GDP growth-6. NEV manufacturing capacity investment stood at RMB 1.64 trillion, up 18% YoY-6.
2026 trajectory: H1 2026 production and sales both exceeded 7 million units, with penetration exceeding 54%. Exports accounted for approximately 31% of total sales — the first time NEV exports exceeded one million units in a single half-year period.
Foreign investment perspective: Manufacturing is now fully open to 100% foreign ownership under the 2024 Negative List reform. For foreign brands considering NEV-related manufacturing in China, the structural opportunity has never been more accessible in regulatory terms — but the competitive window is narrowing.

### 2.2 Lithium-Ion Batteries

2025 metrics: |Activity|Value (RMB bln)|YoY Growth| |---|---|---| |Battery Exports|724|+51%| |Battery Manufacturing Investment|277|+35%| H1 2026 installations: Approximately 340 GWh, up 10% YoY.
Structural shift: LFP (lithium iron phosphate) battery exports surpassed ternary materials in share for the first time in H1 2026 — reflecting global preference for LFP's lower cost and enhanced safety.
Manufacturing investment rebalancing: In 2025, battery projects made up over three-quarters of new manufacturing investment announcements, a stark departure from 2023 and 2024 when solar dominated new investment announcements-11.

### 2.3 Solar PV

2025-2026 context: Solar power investment (generation capacity) reached RMB 1.18 trillion in 2025, up 15% YoY-6. However, solar PV manufacturing investment has seen a steep decline from 2024 levels as capacity rebalancing takes hold-11.
2026 milestone: Solar PV generation capacity is expected to surpass coal in 2026 for the first time-8. This is described as "a historic turning point: photovoltaic power is expected to surpass coal-fired power in 2026 — perhaps the clearest example yet that clean energy has won, regardless of cost, scale, or air quality"-8.
China's global role: China provides 80% of global solar PV modules, contributing to the technology becoming "historically the cheapest power source"-1-8.

## Part III: The Energy Transition — Beyond the "New Three"

### 3.1 Hydrogen — A Growing Frontier

2025 metrics:
China's green hydrogen production capacity exceeds 150,000 tons per year — accounting for more than 50% of the global total-1
540 hydrogen refueling stations have been built — representing 40% of the global share-1
China's hydrogen project commitments stand at $33 billion — the highest globally-4

### 3.2 Coal-to-Clean Transition

Despite being the world's largest coal consumer, China's coal-fired power installed capacity share has steadily decreased from 58.7% in 2015 to 33.1% in H1 2025-1-4.
The 2026 milestone: Solar PV generation is projected to surpass coal generation in 2026 for the first time-8. This marks a fundamental pivot in the structure of China's electricity system.

### 3.3 Emissions — A Potential Peak

Analysts at the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA) have observed that since early 2024, China's carbon emissions have generally plateaued or trended downward, even while electricity consumption continued to rise-8. This suggests China may have already reached or is nearing its emissions peak — a critical milestone for global climate action.

Emerging market trade defense actions: As Chinese NEVs gain share, developing countries may impose their own trade barriers
Countervailing factor: China's clean energy exports to over 200 countries and regions create a broad base of economic partnerships that may moderate trade tensions-1.

## Part VIII: Outlook and Key Signals

### 8.1 What to Watch in H2 2026 and Beyond

Solar PV surpassing coal: This milestone is expected in 2026 — tracking its timing and the market response will indicate the pace of the energy transition-8.
The "New Three" export sustainability: Can the export surge continue beyond the pre-tariff rush, or was it a one-time phenomenon? The answer depends on trade policy and emerging market demand.
Manufacturing investment bottom: The 2025 decline in manufacturing investment may stabilize or rebound. If investment continues to fall, it indicates the capacity build-out phase is truly over. If it stabilizes, the next phase (emerging technologies) is beginning.
Consumption patterns in lower-tier cities: As NEV penetration approaches saturation in Tier 1-2 cities, lower-tier adoption will be the next growth frontier. Charging infrastructure expansion in these areas is the key enabler.
Hydrogen commercialization: With green hydrogen production at 150,000 tons/year (50% of global), the next question is cost competitiveness and application scale-1.

### 8.2 Risk Factors

Investment decline risk: Clean technology manufacturing investment fell 54% in 2025-11. If this trend continues, it signals overcapacity and potential consolidation
Geopolitical risk: The EU anti-subsidy probe, tariffs, and technology export restrictions remain significant uncertainty
Profitability pressure: Intense competition in NEVs, batteries, and solar is squeezing margins across the value chain
Demand transition risk: The shift from policy-driven to market-driven growth is not seamless. Post-subsidy demand softness is real
Infrastructure gaps: Charging and hydrogen refueling infrastructure distribution remains uneven, constraining adoption in lower-tier cities
