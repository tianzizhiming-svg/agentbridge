# Part I: The 2026 Regulatory Landscape – What Has Changed


## 1.1 The Core Development: Judicial Interpretation II (Fa Shi [2025] No. 12)

On September 1, 2025, the Supreme People's Court of China issued its second judicial interpretation on labor disputes, which continues to apply throughout 2026. This interpretation significantly raises the compliance burden for companies operating in China, particularly foreign-invested enterprises, by:

Shifting the focus from contractual form to substantive employment arrangements

Closing multiple employer workarounds that had been used for years

Increasing financial liabilities for non-compliance


## 1.2 Four Key Shifts Affecting Your Operations


| Area | Pre Practice | 2026 Legal Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed-term contracts in Shanghai | Employers could terminate after two consecutive fixed-term contracts | Employers must renew with open-ended contract when conditions are met and employee requests |
| Non-compete clauses | Often applied broadly to all employees | Unenforceable against employees without actual trade secret access; must follow proportionality principle |
| Social insurance waivers | Some companies relied on employee consent to skip or reduce contributions | Any waiver agreement is void; employers liable for back payments and penalties |
| Written contracts | Some employers allowed employees to start work while "finalizing" paperwork | Employer bears burden of proof; limited defences; double salary liability for unsigned periods |


## 1.3 The "Substance Over Form" Principle

Courts will now determine the employing entity by examining actual practices rather than contractual arrangements. The following factors are considered:

Labor contract execution

Actual management and supervision of the employee

Salary payment practices

Social insurance contribution records

Practical implication for group companies: Internal agreements within a corporate group that attempt to allocate liability between subsidiaries will not shield any entity from labor dispute liabilities. In cases of secondment or internal dispatch, the entity responsible for remuneration and benefits during the relevant period must be expressly specified in writing, with the employee's consent.


# Part II: Recruitment and Hiring Compliance


## 2.1 Pre-Hiring Compliance Requirements

Job Postings: Must avoid discriminatory language related to gender, age, marital status, ethnicity, religion, health conditions (including Hepatitis B carrier status), or other protected characteristics.

Background Checks: Under the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), employers must obtain written consent before collecting or processing any candidate's personal information, including data used for background verification.

Salary Benchmarking by City: China has no single national wage. Tier cities (Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen) pay approximately 50% above the national average, while Tier and Tier cities sit 30% below. Your hiring decisions must account for role criticality, location strategy, and long-term workforce structure rather than applying broad national assumptions.


## 2.2 The Written Contract Mandate

Legal requirement: A written labor contract must be signed within 30 days of the employee's start date.

Consequence of failure: If the contract is not signed within this period, the employer must pay double salary for the period during which the employee worked without a written contract.

Key 2026 clarification: The double salary obligation applies even if the unsigned period is less than one month — the calculation is based on actual working days within that period.

Limited defences: The employer bears the burden of proof. The only valid defences are:

Force majeure

Active refusal by the employee to sign (requires documented evidence of the employer's good-faith efforts)

Practical recommendation: Do not allow any employee to begin work without a signed agreement, regardless of their seniority or how "temporary" the role appears. "Trial work" without documentation is extremely risky.


## 2.3 Contract Content Requirements

A compliant labor contract should define:

Employment term (fixed-term, open-ended, or project-based)

Position and responsibilities

Working hours and work hour system type

Workplace location

Compensation structure (base salary, bonus, payment dates)

Benefits and leave entitlement

Probation period (subject to statutory limits)

Confidentiality obligations

Termination conditions

Critical: Foreign contract templates are rarely sufficient. They often fail to reflect China-specific labor rules, mandatory employee protections, local terminology, and enforcement practice. The contract should be in Chinese or include a valid Chinese version.


## 2.4 The Employee Handbook as a Compliance Tool

The labor contract defines the basic relationship, but it is not enough. A well-drafted employee handbook is essential for daily HR operations.

What the handbook should cover:

Workplace rules and attendance requirements

Leave procedures (annual leave, sick leave, maternity leave)

Reimbursement policies

Confidentiality expectations

Disciplinary measures (must be legal, clear, and proportional)

Performance management procedures

Data protection obligations

Termination-related rules

Enforceability requirements: The handbook must be:

Properly drafted in compliance with local law

Communicated to all employees

Acknowledged in writing by each employee

Updated when laws, policies, or operations change

Critical warning: Clauses that impose arbitrary fines, unclear sanctions, or disproportionate penalties may be invalid. If the company later relies on the handbook in a labor dispute, it must show that the rule was legal, clear, communicated, and applied consistently.


# Part III: Employment Contracts – Types and Implications


## 3.1 Fixed-Term Contracts vs. Open-Term Contracts

Fixed-term contracts have a defined end date. After two consecutive fixed-term contracts, the employee gains the right to demand an open-ended contract.

The Shanghai change (2025 onward): Previously, Shanghai courts held that after two consecutive fixed-term contracts, employers retained the option to terminate rather than renew. The new judicial practice eliminates this "termination option": when statutory conditions are met and employees request renewal with open-ended contracts, employers must comply.

National implication: This significantly limits employer discretion in Shanghai and signals a broader national trend toward limiting fixed-term contract use. For employers in other cities, the standard rule still generally allows termination at the end of the second contract, but the Shanghai practice indicates the judicial direction.

Attempted workarounds that no longer work:

Automatic extensions or contractual arrangements still count toward the two-contract threshold

Transferring the employee to another affiliated company with a "fresh" contract will be ignored by courts and considered a continuation of the employment relationship


## 3.2 Project-Based Contracts

Project-based contracts terminate upon project completion. These carry unique risks:

The employer bears the burden of defining and proving project scope

If the project continues beyond the original timeline, the contract may convert to open-term

Courts scrutinize "project-based" designations to ensure they are genuine


## 3.3 Probation Periods (试用期)

Statutory limits based on contract length:


| Contract Term | Maximum Probation |
| --- | --- |
| Less than 3 months | No probation allowed |
| 3 months to 1 year | 1 month |
| 1 year to 3 years | 2 months |
| 3 years or more | 6 months |

Note: An employer may only impose one probation period per employee, regardless of how many contracts are signed.


# Part IV: Non-Compete and Restrictive Covenants


## 4.1 The 2026 Standard: Proportionality and Legitimate Need

The Judicial Interpretation II significantly tightens non-compete enforceability.

Core principle: Non-compete clauses are unenforceable against employees who have not actually accessed trade secrets or confidential information, even if such clauses are included in their contracts.

Specific requirements:

Restrictions must be reasonable in geographic and business scope — sweeping bans will be struck down

The employee must have had actual access to sensitive information — generic confidentiality clauses are insufficient

Non-compete clauses must be tailored to the employee's role, not applied uniformly using templates


## 4.2 Compensation Requirements

Employers must pay at least 30% of the employee's average salary during the restricted period, with a recommendation of 50% for restrictions longer than one year.

Employee rights regarding non-compete:

If the employer fails to pay compensation, the employee may cease performing non-compete obligations

The employer's failure to pay is a valid defence against enforcement

Enforcement consequences:

Employees who breach valid non-compete obligations will bear liability

Damages are capped at five times the compensation actually paid to the employee


## 4.3 Practical Implications for Employers

Audit all existing non-compete agreements to ensure they apply only to employees with actual trade secret access

Eliminate blanket non-compete clauses for junior employees or those in non-sensitive roles

Establish documented trade secret protection measures to support the legitimacy of restrictions

Budget for compensation payments if enforcing non-competes — 30% minimum with 50% recommended for longer periods


# Part V: Working Hours, Overtime, and Leave


## 5.1 Working Hour Systems

China recognizes three types of working hour systems, each with different compliance requirements:


| System | Description | Approval Required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard Work Hour System | 8 hours/day, 5 days/week; overtime payable for work beyond this | Default — no approval needed |
| Comprehensive Work Hour System | Hours calculated over a longer period (month, quarter, or year) | Must be approved by local labor authority |
| Flexible/Non-Fixed Work Hour System | No fixed daily/weekly hours; typically for senior management or sales roles | Must be approved by local labor authority |

Critical warning: Comprehensive and flexible work hour systems should not be used informally simply because a role is senior, mobile, or commercially demanding. Unauthorized use will result in standard overtime liabilities.


## 5.2 Overtime

Work beyond standard hours under a standard work hour system requires statutory overtime payment. Overtime rates are generally:

Weekdays: 150% of regular hourly rate

Weekends: 200% (or compensatory time off)

Public holidays: 300%

Documentation requirement: If your China team regularly works outside standard hours, review whether the current working hour arrangement is legally appropriate and properly documented.


## 5.3 Paid Annual Leave Reform (2026)

The Regulations on Paid Annual Leave for Employees, in effect since 2008, are set for revision in 2026. The forthcoming amendments aim to strengthen enforcement mechanisms and ensure employees can actually take their entitled leave.

Implications: Multinational employers should expect increased scrutiny of leave policies and internal enforcement practices. The reform signals that China is moving toward more active enforcement of employee leave entitlements.


## 5.4 Maternity Leave

National minimum: Female employees are entitled to 98 days of maternity leave, of which 15 days may be taken before delivery. Extra leave is granted for dystocia (15 additional days) and multiple births (15 days for each additional child).

Local variations: Cities such as Beijing and Shanghai extend this to 158 days total (98 days national + 60 days local).

Paternity leave: Varies significantly by city. Employers must check local regulations.


# Part VI: Payroll, Social Insurance, and the Housing Fund


## 6.1 Social Insurance (五险) — Non-Waivable

Legal basis: The Judicial Interpretation II confirms that any agreement to waive statutory social insurance contributions — whether initiated by the employer or the employee — is invalid as it undermines public interests.

This applies regardless of:

Bilateral consent between employer and employee

Employee's own request or commitment to waive

Private agreements

Consequences of non-compliance:

Employees may resign immediately and claim damages if contributions are missing or incomplete

Courts will award additional damages even if the company later pays arrears

One medium-sized firm that had underpaid for years was hit with multi-million RMB back payments, penalties, and employee compensation


## 6.2 Component Breakdown


| Component | Employee Contribution | Employer Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pension (养老保险) | 8% of salary | 16% of salary |
| Medical (医疗保险) | ~2% of salary | ~9% of salary |
| Unemployment (失业保险) | 0.5% | 0.5% |
| Work Injury (工伤保险) | None | 0.2.9% |
| Maternity (生育保险) | None | ~0.5% |
| Housing Fund (住房公积金) | 5% | 5% |

Note: Local variations apply to percentages.


## 6.3 Housing Fund (住房公积金)

The housing fund is also statutory. Upon leaving China, employees can withdraw the full balance (employee + employer contributions).

For permanent residents: Employers must pay both social insurance and housing fund for permanent residents in full-time employment relationships.


## 6.4 Payroll Compliance Checklist

Employers are responsible for:

Correct salary calculation

Individual income tax (IIT) withholding

Social insurance contributions (employee + employer portions)

Housing fund contributions

Pay-slip records

Timely reporting to tax and social insurance authorities

Practical point: Local payroll costs differ according to the employee's city, hukou location, salary level, and applicable contribution bases. Before approving a hire, review the local employment costs and statutory contribution requirements in the city where the employee will be based.


# Part VII: Termination, Severance, and Reinstatement


## 7.1 Termination by Employer — Legal Grounds

With cause termination (serious misconduct, criminal conviction, etc.): No severance.

Without cause termination: Statutory severance applies — one month's salary for each year of service, with partial years pro-rated.

Illegal termination: If the employer terminates without legal basis and without following proper procedures, the employee may be entitled to:

Double the statutory severance (2N), OR

Reinstatement + back salary from termination date


## 7.2 Reinstatement — New Limitations

Illegally dismissed workers can demand reinstatement. However, courts now define when reinstatement is not feasible:

The employer is bankrupt or liquidated

The employee has already retired and receives a pension

The employee has joined another company and returning would be unreasonable

If reinstatement is not feasible, the employer must pay double the statutory severance.


## 7.3 Back Salary During Litigation

If an employee challenges a dismissal and later wins, the employer must pay full salary from the termination date until the day before reinstatement.

Financial exposure: Because labor lawsuits in China can last months or even years, this exposes businesses to large liabilities. Even where reinstatement is not ultimately ordered, wage arrears during litigation can represent a major expense.


## 7.4 Resignation by Employee

Notice period: Employees must give 30 days' written notice for resignation.

Employer's obligations upon resignation:

Issue release letter (离职证明) — legally required

Cancel the employee's work permit (for foreign employees)

Issue final salary settlement including unpaid wages, bonuses, and unused annual leave compensation

Recent case: A 2026 case involving a foreign employee in Xining successfully recovered compensation and severance through arbitration in 7 days — the tribunal used translation tools and bilingual mediation to reach a settlement.


# Part VIII: Dispute Resolution – The Arbitration Pathway


## 8.1 Arbitration as the Mandatory First Step

Labor arbitration is the mandatory first step before any court litigation. Appeals to court are only available after an arbitration award is issued.


## 8.2 Who Can Apply

Foreign employees who have disputes with their employers arising from labor-related issues such as:

Working hours and rest/leave

Social insurance

Welfare benefits

Labor protection

Termination disputes

Wage and compensation claims

Legal basis: The Labor Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Law of the PRC provides that foreign employees with valid work permits are protected under Chinese labor law and may apply for arbitration.


## 8.3 Where to Apply

Arbitration applications should be submitted to the labor dispute arbitration committee in the location where:

The employee works

The employer is registered, or

The employer's main office is located


## 8.4 Time Limit (Critical: 1-Year Statute of Limitations)

The time limit for applying for arbitration is one year, calculated from the date when the employee becomes aware (or should have been aware) that their rights have been infringed.

Two critical exceptions:

Unpaid wages during employment: The one-year limit does not apply while the employee is still employed

Termination: If the employment contract has been terminated, the application must be made within one year from the date of termination

Procedural trap for employers: The statute of limitations defence must be raised at the arbitration stage. If the employer fails to raise it then, the defence is lost forever, including in appeal or retrial proceedings.


## 8.5 Required Documents

For arbitration applications, you will need:

Original application for arbitration (3 copies)

Copy of passport

Copy of work permit

Copy of labor contract

Employer's company registration information (obtainable from the market supervision bureau)

Evidence supporting your claims (salary slips, offer letters, email communications)


## 8.6 The Process


| Step | Timeline | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Application filing | Day 1 | Free of charge |
| Arbitrator contact for mediation (optional) | 2 weeks | The employee may choose to participate or proceed directly to hearing |
| Official arbitration hearing | 2 weeks after mediation fails | Evidence presented |
| Arbitration decision issued | Within 60 days of filing | Binding unless appealed |
| Appeal to court | Within 15 days of decision | Either party may appeal |


## 8.7 Costs

Filing for arbitration is free of charge

Legal fees vary (consult with specialized labor lawyers)

Legal fees are not covered by the employer unless mutually agreed upon


## 8.8 Special Considerations for Foreign Employees

Foreign employees face three distinct challenges in arbitration:

Legal understanding differences: Foreign employees often lack systematic understanding of Chinese labor law, arbitration procedures, and evidence rules, resulting in inadequate claim presentation

Language and communication barriers: Translation issues and cultural differences in describing facts can create significant misunderstandings

Expectation differences: Foreign employees' expectations of compensation, leave, and termination procedures differ from Chinese judicial standards, making mediation difficult

Recommendation: Engage a lawyer with bilingual capability and experience in foreign-related labor disputes. In a 2026 foreign teacher case in Dongguan, bilingual legal representation successfully bridged these gaps and achieved a written mediation agreement after six hours of negotiation.


# Part IX: Data Compliance and Privacy


## 9.1 The Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL)

PIPL imposes significant restrictions on how employers handle employee data, especially when exporting it overseas.

What applies to you:

Consent requirement: Employers must obtain employee consent before transferring personal data (passport information, health data, performance data) to a parent company or IT system outside China

Biometric data: Special protections apply to fingerprints, facial recognition data, and other biometric information

Third-party sharing: Data sharing with vendors outside China requires additional compliance measures


## 9.2 Quantitative Thresholds

Standard contract filing: If the employer exports personal data of 100,000+ individuals (cumulative) in a year, they must file a standard contract with the local cyberspace authority

Security assessment: If the employer exports personal data of 1 million+ individuals (cumulative), they must undergo a full security assessment

Practical implication: Your employer cannot automatically sync your work file, vacation records, performance reviews, and payroll data to a global HR system without satisfying these requirements.


## 9.3 Employee Rights Under PIPL

Right to access: Request copies of all personal data held

Right to deletion: Request deletion when data is no longer needed for its original purpose

Right to portability: Request data transfer to another platform (where technically feasible)

Right to object: Object to processing for direct marketing

These rights are legally enforceable in China.


# Part X: Workforce Structuring – Strategic Considerations


## 10.1 "Measured Hiring" as the 2026 Norm

2026 is defined not by rapid expansion but by measured hiring, disciplined workforce planning, and heightened compliance expectations. Companies are reassessing how they recruit, structure, and retain talent, while advanced AI-driven HR technologies accelerate transformation.


## 10.2 Local Talent Acquisition

Salary benchmarking by city: Hiring decisions now hinge on role criticality, location strategy, and long-term workforce structure rather than broad national assumptions.

The fastest way to build a local team in an unfamiliar city is to work with a local recruitment partner who knows that city's talent market, then employ first hires through an Employer of Record while validating the market — onboarding in days rather than the months a WFOE takes.


## 10.3 The Super-Aged Workforce

China's gradual retirement age extension began on January 1, 2025. To address the rights of workers beyond statutory retirement age, MOHRSS released a draft regulation covering "super-aged workers," including those who retire early and return to work. The regulation mandates:

Written employment agreements

Minimum wage compliance

Proper payment procedures

Final implementation is expected in 2026, creating new compliance obligations for employers of older workers.

# Part XII: Common Pitfalls to Avoid

Pitfall 1: "We Have a Global Employment Contract Template"

Reality: Foreign contract templates rarely reflect China-specific requirements. A standard labor contract is subject to mandatory Chinese legal provisions that cannot be waived. "Trial work" without a signed Chinese contract exposes the company to double salary liability.

Pitfall 2: "We Have a Mutual Agreement to Skip Social Insurance"

Reality: Any agreement to waive social insurance contributions is void and unenforceable. Even if the employee requested the waiver in writing, the employer remains liable for back payments, penalties, and employee compensation.

Pitfall 3: "Our Non-Compete Clause Covers All Employees"

Reality: Non-compete clauses are unenforceable against employees who have not actually accessed trade secrets. Sweeping clauses will be struck down. You must prove the employee had access to sensitive information.

Pitfall 4: "We Can Terminate After Two Fixed-Term Contracts"

Reality (Shanghai, 2026): This is no longer true. Employers in Shanghai must renew with open-ended contracts when the employee requests it. Other cities may follow.

Pitfall 5: "We Don't Need a Written Employee Handbook"

Reality: The employee handbook is a key compliance tool. Without it, enforcing discipline and managing terminations is significantly more difficult. The handbook must be properly drafted, communicated, and acknowledged.

Pitfall 6: "We Can Rely on the Statute of Limitations at Trial"

Reality: The statute of limitations defence must be raised at the arbitration stage. Failure to raise it there means the defence is lost forever, including in subsequent court proceedings.
