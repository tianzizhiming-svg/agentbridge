# Part I: Your Legal Rights — What the Law Actually Says


## 1.1 You Are Protected By Law, Equally

Under the Consumer Protection Law of the People's Republic of China, foreign nationals in China enjoy the status as consumers when purchasing and using goods or receiving services for personal needs. They are entitled to the same protections as Chinese citizens .

Your 9 Legal Rights Under the Consumer Protection Law :


| # | Right | What This Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Safety of Person and Property | Products and services must not harm you or your property |
| 2 | True Knowledge | Sellers must provide complete, accurate information — in Chinese — about what you are buying |
| 3 | Free Choice | You cannot be forced to buy anything |
| 4 | Fair Transaction | No price gouging, no forced bundling |
| 5 | Compensation | If a product causes harm or loss, you are entitled to compensation |
| 6 | Consumer Organizations | You can join or form groups to protect consumer rights |
| 7 | Consumer Information | You have the right to obtain information about consumer protections |
| 8 | Respect for Personality | Your dignity and customs must be respected |
| 9 | Supervision | You can supervise products, services, and government enforcement |


## 1.2 The "7-Day Unconditional Return" Right

For online purchases, the Consumer Protection Law grants you the right to return items within 7 days of receipt without giving any reason — for most products. This right applies to you regardless of your nationality.

Exceptions (no return right):

Custom-made goods

Perishable goods (food, fresh produce)

Digital downloads (software, music, e-books)

Newspapers, magazines

Other items specified by law

For physical stores: There is no general legal right to return items without cause. However, stores may offer return policies voluntarily.


## 1.3 Food Safety — Enhanced Protections

Chinese food safety laws impose strict liability on sellers. If you buy food that is unsafe, spoiled, or mislabeled, you have enhanced rights:

You can claim 10 times the purchase price as compensation (minimum RMB 1,000)

The seller bears the burden of proving the food was safe

Foreign food products must have Chinese labeling — if they don't, they are considered non-compliant

Real case: In a 2026 case involving a foreign consumer in China using social media to buy food from a seller in another province, the consumer was advised to preserve all chat records, payment receipts, and photographs of the product, then file a complaint through the local 12315 channel . The law protected them equally.


# Part II: The 12315 System — Your Primary Enforcement Tool


## 2.1 What Is 12315?

12315 is China's national consumer complaint hotline and online platform, operated by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). It is the first and most accessible line of defense for any consumer dispute.

For foreign consumers, 12315 is available and fully operational. In multiple documented cases, foreign nationals have successfully used 12315 to resolve disputes:

In Inner Mongolia, a Mongolian national with only 5 days left on their visa used the 12315 system to resolve a laptop repair dispute within 3 days

In Beijing, a foreign resident used the 12345 (citizen service) hotline to resolve a rental dispute

Legal professionals confirm that foreign consumers have equal access to this system


## 2.2 How to File a Complaint

Method 1: Phone Call

Dial 12315 (nationwide) or add the local area code for city-specific services

In some regions, the service includes non-Chinese languages. The Inner Mongolia system, for example, has dedicated Mongolian-language operators for cross-border consumers

Warning: Most operators speak only Chinese. Have your issue summarized in writing with key keywords.

Method 2: Online Platform

Use the "全国12315平台" (National 12315 Platform) — available as a WeChat mini-program, Alipay mini-program, and website

Download the App: "全国12315"

Complaint can be filed entirely online with attached evidence

Method 3: In-Person

Visit your local Market Supervision Administration (市场监督管理局) office

Bring your passport and evidence


## 2.3 Required Information for Filing

To file an effective complaint, you need:


| Information | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Your name and contact information | Passport name and phone number |
| Business name | The exact registered name of the business |
| Business registration number | If available (统一社会信用代码) |
| Transaction details | Date, amount, item/service description |
| Evidence | Receipts, photos, chat screenshots, contracts |
| Your demand | What resolution you are seeking (refund, repair, compensation) |

Critical evidence preservation: Lawyers emphasize that all chat records (WeChat, social media), payment receipts, and photographs must be preserved immediately. For digital evidence, the official recommendation is to notarize chat logs before they are deleted or altered .


## 2.4 Timeline and Process


| Step | Timeline | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complaint filed | Day 1 | Online or phone |
| Initial review | 7 days | Business must respond to complaint |
| Mediation initiated | 30 days | If both parties agree |
| Outcome | 45 days | Mediation agreement or referral to other channels |

The law suggests businesses respond within 7 business days to consumer complaints . In practice, this timeline is tracked and enforced.

Real case example (Rental Dispute, Beijing): A foreign woman named Chloe filed a complaint through the 12345 hotline regarding a rental dispute with her apartment management company. The Beijing Fengtai District authorities mobilized quickly, used translation tools and English SMS to communicate with her, and resolved the issue with a refund within days .


# Part III: Rental Disputes — Your Legal Rights and Recourse


## 3.1 The Legal Framework: What Protects You

Rental disputes are the most common legal issue for foreigners in China. The framework is:

Civil Code of China: Provides the general legal basis for rental contracts

Consumer Protection Law: Applies if the rental is for personal living needs

Local housing regulations: Each city has specific rules

Key principles:

"Sale does not break the lease" (买卖不破租赁): If the landlord sells the property, your lease continues — you cannot be forced to move

Security deposit rules: Landlords can only deduct for legitimate reasons (unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear and tear) — they cannot keep your deposit for trivial reasons

Written contracts: While oral contracts can be binding, written contracts are strongly preferred by courts


## 3.2 The Language Barrier Problem — and Legal Implications

Many foreign tenants sign leases that are only in Chinese. Courts have held that this fact alone does not make the contract invalid — but it is recognized as a practical challenge.

Beijing real case (2026): A foreign woman, Chloe, signed a lease that was only in Chinese. When she tried to move out, she had a dispute over water fees and other charges. The Fengtai District Housing Authority mediated the dispute, and the property management company agreed to a refund. The city government noted that while the contract being Chinese-only was not illegal, it was a barrier that needed to be addressed through enhanced services for foreign tenants .

What to do if you signed a Chinese-only lease:

Request a translation (not legally binding but helps you understand terms)

Keep all communication with the landlord in writing (WeChat messages are acceptable)

Document the condition of the property with photos and videos upon move-in


## 3.3 The 24-Hour Registration — A Legal Obligation with Consumer Rights Implications

You must register your accommodation within 24 hours of moving in. This is not a suggestion — it is a legal requirement under the Exit & Entry Administration Law .

If you are in a hotel, they handle this automatically

If you are in a private rental or homestay, you must register at the local police station (派出所)

Failure to register can result in fines (RMB 500,000) and complications with visa renewal

Documents needed for police registration:

Original passport

Lease agreement or written invitation letter from the host

Host's Chinese ID copy

Property ownership certificate copy (the host must provide this)


## 3.4 Dispute Resolution Pathways for Rentals


| Pathway | Timeline | When to Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct negotiation | 1 weeks | Always the first step |
| 12315 / 12345 | 30 days | When negotiation fails; housing authorities often intervene |
| Community Mediation Center (综治中心) | 1 weeks | The first formal option for disputes; often free |
| Labor/Personnel Arbitration | 30 days | If the dispute involves employment-related accommodation |
| Court Litigation | 3 months | Last resort; requires legal representation |

Real case (Zhejiang, 2026): A foreign man had his lease expire while he was overseas, leaving his belongings in the apartment. Upon returning, the landlord had removed his items to storage and demanded back rent. The town Mediation Center (综治中心) intervened, using translation software to bridge the language gap. After a series of meetings, they reached a settlement: the foreign national's items were used as partial payment for the outstanding rent .

Real case (Yangzhou, 2026): Two Hong Kong residents had a rental dispute that involved pet damage claims. The dispute had been through police intervention twice without resolution. The local Arbitration Commission (仲裁委员会) used a joint mechanism with police and the Mediation Center to bring the parties together. Within a single session, the agreement was signed and payment was made on the spot .


## 3.5 Deposit Deductions — What Is Legitimate

Landlords cannot deduct for:

Normal wear and tear

Cleaning (unless extraordinary)

Utility bills not specified in the contract

"Processing fees" or "documentation fees"

Landlords can deduct for:

Unpaid rent

Property damage beyond normal wear and tear

Unauthorized subletting or modifications

Practical recommendation: Take detailed photos and videos of the property upon move-in and move-out. Share them with the landlord in writing. If the landlord withholds your deposit without justification, you have grounds for a formal complaint.


# Part IV: Contract Disputes — When You Are Tied to a Chinese-Only Contract


## 4.1 What the Law Says

Chinese law allows contracts to be in Chinese-only. However, if you were induced to sign a contract through misrepresentation, fraud, or without understanding its material terms, the contract may be partially or wholly invalid.

Practically: Courts expect you to understand what you sign. "I didn't read it" is not a valid defence. If you cannot read Chinese, you should:

Have the contract professionally translated

Ask the other party to provide a translation

Seek legal advice before signing


## 4.2 Real Cases — and Lessons

Beijing Rental Dispute (2026): A foreign woman signed a lease in Chinese only. When the issue was escalated to the Fengtai District authorities, they noted that while there was no legal violation, the situation highlighted the need for better services for foreign tenants. The authorities resolved the dispute by negotiating with the property management company and securing a refund .

Foreign Consumer Food Purchase (2026): A foreign consumer who bought food via a social media platform was advised by lawyers to preserve all communications, payment receipts, and photographs, and to file a complaint with 12315 in the seller's jurisdiction. The legal advice confirmed that the consumer has equal rights, regardless of nationality .

Medical Tourism Risk Warning (2026): A Russian consumer advisory agency warned that Chinese dental clinic contracts signed by Russian medical tourists are in Chinese and in many cases the patients do not understand the terms. In case of dispute, legal action must be taken in Chinese courts, which requires a Chinese lawyer and involves court costs, language barriers, and lengthy procedures .

Lesson: For contracts of significant value (leases, medical treatment, major purchases), professional translation or legal advice before signing is essential.


# Part V: The Legal Process — Arbitration, Mediation, and Courts


## 5.1 The Three-Tier Dispute Resolution System

Tier 1: Mediation and Administrative Channels

12315 (consumer complaints)

12345 (citizen hotline)

Community Mediation Centers (综治中心)

Local Housing Authorities (房管局)

Tier 2: Arbitration

Arbitration Commissions (仲裁委员会) offer faster, more efficient dispute resolution than courts

Arbitration is voluntary — both parties must agree to participate

Arbitration decisions are binding

Process can be completed in 30 days

Tier 3: Court Litigation

Court should be considered last resort

Requires filing a lawsuit in the People's Court with jurisdiction over the dispute

Must be represented by a Chinese lawyer (you cannot represent yourself if you are a foreign national — the Civil Procedure Law requires foreign parties to appoint a Chinese lawyer as their representative)

Timeframe: 3 months


## 5.2 Court Litigation — Step-by-Step

Documents required for filing a lawsuit:


| Document | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Statement of Claim | Must be in Chinese; can be written by a law firm if you cannot write in Chinese |
| Plaintiff Identity Documents | Copy of your passport |
| Power of Attorney | If using legal representation |
| Evidence | Receipts, invoices, contracts, shipping documents, chat screenshots, product evaluations |

The Foreign Lawyer Rule: You must appoint a Chinese lawyer for any court proceeding. The Civil Procedure Law states: "For foreigners, stateless persons, foreign enterprises, and organizations that need to appoint a lawyer to represent them in a People's Court for prosecution or response, they must appoint a lawyer from the People's Republic of China" .

Costs: Court fees are calculated based on the claim amount. Legal fees depend on the lawyer and case complexity (typically RMB 5,000,000 and above).


## 5.3 The Statute of Limitations — A Critical Deadline

The general statute of limitations for civil claims in China is 3 years from when you become aware (or should have become aware) of your rights being infringed.

For consumer disputes:

The Consumer Protection Law gives consumers a claim period that varies by type of claim

For product defects causing personal injury, the claim period is typically 2 years

For contract disputes (including rental and sale agreements), the standard 3-year period applies

Warning: If you do not take action within the statute of limitations, your claim is barred. Courts will not hear it.

# Part VII: Cross-Border and Online Transactions — Special Considerations


## 7.1 Buying from Foreign Platforms While in China

If you are physically in China and buy from a foreign e-commerce platform, the transaction is subject to:

The platform's terms of service (often governed by the platform's home country law)

Chinese customs and import regulations (if the product crosses the border)

Chinese consumer law if the seller has a physical presence in China or specifically targets Chinese consumers

Practical advice: For returns and refunds, follow the platform's procedures first. If that fails, you may still have rights under Chinese law if the seller operates in China.


## 7.2 Buying from Chinese Platforms While Abroad

If you are outside China and buy from a Chinese platform (Taobao, JD.com, etc.):

You are a consumer protected by Chinese law if the seller is in China

Your primary recourse is through the platform's dispute resolution system

If that fails, you can file a 12315 complaint — but enforcement outside China is limited

The court route requires bringing the case in China, which is operationally difficult if you are not in the country

Real case: A foreign consumer who bought food via WeChat from a seller in another Chinese province was advised by lawyers to:

Preserve all chat records and payment receipts

Contact the seller directly for a refund

If the seller refuses, file a 12315 complaint in the seller's jurisdiction

If that fails, consider court action in the seller's city


## 7.3 Shopping via Social Media Platforms

Shopping via WeChat, Little Red Book (小红书), Douyin (抖音), and other social media platforms is increasingly common. The legal protections are the same as for any e-commerce transaction — but enforcement is sometimes harder because sellers may be unregistered individuals rather than formal businesses.

Risk mitigation:

Verify the seller's real name and contact information before paying

Keep comprehensive records of all communications

Use platform payment systems rather than direct transfer (some platforms hold funds and can mediate)

For expensive items, request a formal receipt/invoice (发票)


# Part VIII: Business Compliance for Foreign Enterprises — The Other Side

If you are a foreign business operating in China, the Consumer Protection Law imposes significant obligations on you. These are not optional.


## 8.1 What You Must Do as a Business

Obligation 1: Information Transparency

Your product labels and marketing materials must be in Chinese

You cannot make misleading claims — "best," "number one," "100% natural" must be backed by evidence

All terms and conditions, warranty policies, and return policies must be clearly communicated

Obligation 2: Compliance with Standard Terms

Unfair standard terms (e.g., "no returns," "no refunds") are void if they unreasonably limit consumer rights

If your contract disclaims liability for harm caused by your product, it will be struck down

Obligation 3: Consumer Complaint Management

You must have a system in place to receive, process, and respond to consumer complaints

The law suggests a 7-business-day response window

Each complaint should be documented, tracked, and resolved

Obligation 4: Product Recall

You are responsible for recalling defective products that pose safety risks

The consumer protection law includes recall obligations

Real case (Shanghai foreign cosmetics company): A foreign cosmetics company's product labels initially used only scientific ingredient names without Chinese explanations. Consumers complained that the information was "hidden." The company redesigned labels to include simple Chinese explanations of each ingredient's function — turning compliance into a marketing point that emphasized "trust through knowledge" .


## 8.2 Penalties for Non-Compliance

Consumer protection enforcement is a significant risk:

Administrative penalties by Market Supervision Administrations

Forced product recalls and destruction of non-compliant products

Public disclosure of violations — which can damage brand reputation permanently

Consumer lawsuits and compensation claims

Digital risk: A consumer complaint that goes viral on Little Red Book (小红书) or Dianping (大众点评) can cause irreparable brand damage within hours .

# Part X: Quick Reference — What to Do in Common Scenarios

Scenario 1: Product Defective or Damaged

Stop using the product and preserve it for inspection

Take clear photographs of the defect and any damage

Preserve the receipt and any packaging

Contact the seller in writing (WeChat, email) with evidence

If the seller refuses a refund or exchange:

Call 12315 (or file online)

Provide all evidence to the 12315 system

Wait for the complaint to be processed (usually 7 days for initial response)

Mediation will be attempted (30 days)

Scenario 2: Unfair Termination of Housing Lease

Do not vacate immediately — you have legal rights

Review your lease (if you have one) for termination provisions

Document the landlord's communication regarding the termination

Contact the local Housing Authority (via 12345) for mediation

If the landlord refuses to return your deposit:

Use 12315 to file a consumer complaint

If that fails, the local Mediation Center (综治中心) can help

As a last resort, court action through a Chinese lawyer

Scenario 3: Defective Food or Unsafe Service

Preserve the food (or evidence of the service)

Document all communication with the business

File a 12315 complaint — food safety cases are prioritized

If you have suffered physical harm, also seek medical attention and keep the medical records as evidence

In severe cases, contact police (110) and your home country's embassy
