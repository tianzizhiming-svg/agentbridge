# Foreword

You have been admitted to a Chinese university. Your admission letter is in your inbox. You have heard that you need a "student visa," but you are not sure which one. Your friends have told you to "just get the visa" and figure out the rest later.

That is a dangerous approach.

This is not a general overview of studying abroad. This is the operational manual that covers: which student visa you actually need and how to get it, what the JW201/JW202/DQ forms are and why you cannot apply without them, how to download and verify these forms correctly, why the X1 visa must be converted to a residence permit within 30 days, what to do if your accommodation registration is not handled automatically, where to go to avoid overstay fines of RMB 500 per day, and how to stay legally in China for the full duration of your studies.

Every recommendation in this guide is grounded in official Chinese government procedures, university guidelines, and actual legal requirements. This is the manual you need before you board your flight.


# Part I: Understanding Your Visa Options


## 1.1 X1 Visa — Long-Term Study (More Than 180 Days)

Who needs it: Students enrolled in degree programs (Bachelor's, Master's, PhD), one-year exchange programs, or any study program lasting longer than 180 days .

Key characteristics:

Valid for single entry into China

Allows stay of 30 days after arrival

Must be converted to a Residence Permit within those 30 days

Can be extended while in China (subject to university verification)

The critical 30-day rule: The X1 visa is a transit visa. It does not grant you permission to remain in China for your entire study period. The Residence Permit is what legally allows your long-term stay . Missing the 30-day deadline results in overstay fines and potential visa cancellation.


## 1.2 X2 Visa — Short-Term Study (Up to 180 Days)

Who needs it: Students enrolled in short-term programs — language courses, semester-long exchange programs, summer/winter schools, visiting scholars .

Key characteristics:

Valid for stay up to 180 days

Cannot be converted to a Residence Permit

Cannot be extended beyond the allowed period

Students must leave China and re-enter if they need to stay longer

For visiting scholars and short-term researchers: If you are coming for a few weeks, you may need an F visa (for exchange, visit, study activities) rather than X2 — check with your host institution .


## 1.3 What NOT to Do

Do not enter China on a Tourist Visa (L Visa) with the intention of switching to a student visa.

If you enter on an L Visa, you may be required to leave China and re-enter to obtain the correct visa status — which is expensive, time-consuming, and operationally complicated .


# Part II: Pre-Departure — Documents You Must Obtain


## 2.1 The JW201/JW202/DQ Forms — Your Most Important Documents

These forms are issued by the Chinese Ministry of Education and are required for your X visa application . Without them, you cannot apply for a student visa.


| Form | Who Needs It | Issued By |
| --- | --- | --- |
| JW201 | CSC Scholarship students | CSC application system |
| JW202 | Full-time degree students, one-year exchange programs | University, via Study in China system |
| DQ | Short-term students (one semester, non-degree) | University, via Study in China system |

How to obtain and verify them:

Your university applies for the form on your behalf after your admission is confirmed

You receive an email from the Study in China system (studyinchina.edu.cn) with a download link

You must download and print the form — on a computer, not a smartphone/tablet (the system is not mobile-compatible)

Verify the form on the Study in China website after downloading

IMPORTANT: The system only allows you to download the form a maximum of 10 times. Make sure you save the file securely .

IMPORTANT: Use the same email address you used for your university application. The system will not recognize a different email address .

How to confirm your form type:

JW201: CSC scholarship students

JW202: Full-time Bachelor's, Master's, PhD, and one-year visiting/exchange programs

DQ: Non-degree Chinese language programs and one-semester exchange programs


## 2.2 Admission Notice

You will receive an electronic copy of your Admission Notice with the university seal. You must:

Download it from your university's application system

Print it in color

Use the printed color copy for your visa application

Note: Only materials provided through the university's official application system and official contact email are considered genuine and valid .


## 2.3 Passport Requirements


| Requirement | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum validity | 6 months beyond the intended stay |
| Blank visa pages | At least one blank page |
| Photocopy | Copy of the passport information page |


## 2.4 Visa Application Form and Photo

Complete the Visa Application Form of the People's Republic of China online

Most applicants need to make an online appointment via the AVAS (Appointment for Visa Application Submission) website

One recent color passport photo, bareheaded, full-faced, against a light background


## 2.5 Proof of Legal Stay (For Applications Outside Your Home Country)

If you are not applying for your visa in your home country, you need:

Original and copy of your valid documents proving legal stay, residence, work, or study in the current country


# Part III: Preparing for Arrival — What to Bring


## 3.1 What to Pack (Practical Advice)

Electronic devices:

Laptop and accessories

Power adapters (China uses 220V, Type A/I sockets — flat two-pin or three-pin)

Portable charger, phone charger

Bedding:

Bed sheet, duvet cover, pillowcase, duvet, mattress pad

Toiletries:

Slippers, bath towel, towel, toothbrush, toothpaste

Clothing:

China has vast climate differences — research your university's geographic location and pack accordingly

Medications:

Common drugs: respiratory and digestive medicines, painkillers, anti-inflammatory drugs, mosquito repellent, moisturizer

WARNING: Opium, morphine, heroin, marijuana, and other addictive narcotics are strictly prohibited from being brought into China

Cash:

Bring some RMB cash to cover basic expenses from airport to school and daily necessities

Pro tip: Many items can be purchased on campus or at nearby shopping malls after arrival — you don't need to bring everything from home .


## 3.2 What NOT to Bring

All types of weapons, simulated weapons, controlled knives, ammunition, and explosives

Prohibited narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances


# Part IV: Arrival — The First 30 Days (Critical Window)


## 4.1 The 30-Day Countdown

Your X1 visa is valid for 30 days from the date of entry.

Within these 30 days, you must:

Register your accommodation (within 24 hours of arrival)

Complete the physical examination (if required)

Apply for the Residence Permit at the local Exit & Entry Administration

Missing any of these deadlines puts you in overstay status, triggering daily fines and potential visa complications.


## 4.2 Day 1: Accommodation Registration (24-Hour Rule)

Legal requirement: All foreign nationals must register their accommodation within 24 hours of arrival.

Case A: Staying in a University Dormitory

The university typically handles registration during check-in

Keep the registration slip — you will need it for your Residence Permit application

You may not need to visit a police station

Case B: Staying in Off-Campus Rental or Private Accommodation

You must register at the local police station (派出所) in person

What to bring:

Original passport

Lease agreement (or written invitation letter from host)

Host's Chinese ID copy

Property ownership certificate copy (the host must provide this)

Failure to register triggers a fine of RMB 500,000 and will complicate your Residence Permit application.


## 4.3 Physical Examination

Who needs it: Students applying for the X1 visa must undergo a physical examination at a designated Chinese health center.

What to bring:

Original passport

The physical exam form from your home country (if any)

2 passport photos

Fasting (for blood tests)

What they test:

General physical examination

Chest X-ray

Blood tests

ECG

Timeline: Results take 3 working days. You receive a Health Certificate (健康证明书) — valid for 6 months.

Cost: Approximately RMB 500, depending on the city.


## 4.4 The Residence Permit Application (THE MOST CRITICAL STEP)

Deadline: Must be completed within 30 days of entry — before your X1 visa expires.

What you need:


| Document | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Completed residence permit application form | Available at the bureau or online |
| Original passport | Minimum 6 months validity |
| Admission Notice | Original or certified copy |
| JW201/JW202 form | Original |
| Health Certificate | Issued by the designated health center |
| Temporary accommodation registration slip | From the university or police station |
| 1 passport-size photos | Chinese photo specifications |
| University letter | Confirming enrollment and accommodation |
| Registration fee payment | Typically RMB 800 for 1-year permits |

Processing time: 5 working days. You receive a passport sticker (Residence Permit) with the validity period.

Length of Residence Permit: Usually issued for 1 year initially, renewable annually, based on your academic program duration.

Critical warning: During this 30-day window, your legal stay is in a transitional state. Do not travel internationally during this period unless absolutely necessary. Re-entry after travel resets the clock and may cause complications.


## 4.5 Visa/Residence Permit Extension

For X1 visa holders: The Residence Permit is renewable annually, subject to university verification of your continued enrollment. Renewal applications should be filed approximately 30 days before expiry.

For X2 visa holders: The visa cannot be extended. You must leave China before the 180-day period expires.


# Part V: Legal Obligations During Your Stay


## 5.1 Residence Registration — Not Just for Arrival

Every change of address requires a new police station registration within 24 hours

Visits to other cities — some hotels handle this automatically; for private accommodation, you must register locally


## 5.2 Work Restrictions

Foreign students on X1 visas are generally prohibited from off-campus work.

Unauthorized work is considered illegal employment and can result in:

Fines

Visa cancellation

Deportation

Internship exceptions: Some universities allow internships if approved by the school and the immigration authorities. You must obtain formal approval before starting any internship. The employer must have a valid business license, and a formal agreement must be in place.


## 5.3 Overstay Penalties

If you overstay your visa or Residence Permit:

Fine: Approximately RMB 500 per day

Visa cancellation: In severe cases, your visa may be cancelled

Deportation: For significant or repeated overstays

Future visa impacts: A history of overstay can complicate future Chinese visa applications

Proactive steps:

Check your visa/residence permit expiry date at least 30 days in advance

Apply for renewal before the expiry date

Do not wait until the last week — processing takes 5 working days

## 6.2 Opening a Bank Account

Why you need it: For receiving scholarship payments, paying tuition, and everyday transactions in China.

Documents needed:

Original passport

Residence Permit (or X1/X2 visa with valid stay)

Accommodation registration slip

Phone number (you need a Chinese phone number)

Processing time: 30 minutes at the bank branch.


## 6.3 Getting a Local SIM Card

Documents needed: Original passport
Options:

China Mobile, China Unicom, China Telecom — all offer student plans

eSIM (international data eSIMs like Airalo, Nomad) bypass the Great Firewall automatically but cost more per GB

Local SIM — cheaper but subject to the Great Firewall (requires VPN)

Recommendation: Get a local SIM for everyday use and an international eSIM as backup.

# Part IX: Practical Tips and Common Pitfalls

Pitfall 1: "I'll Get the JW Form When I Arrive"

Reality: The JW202/DQ form must be obtained and verified before you apply for the visa — i.e., before you arrive. It is issued by the university through the Study in China system, not at the airport .

Pitfall 2: "I Don't Need the Residence Permit — My X1 Visa Is Valid for 30 Days"

Reality: The X1 visa is only valid for entry and a 30-day transition period. Your legal status becomes invalid after 30 days unless you convert to the Residence Permit . Overstaying triggers fines and visa cancellation.

Pitfall 3: "I'll Register My Accommodation Later"

Reality: The 24-hour accommodation registration is a legal requirement with penalties . It is also a prerequisite for your Residence Permit application — if you don't have the registration slip, you cannot complete the permit process.

Pitfall 4: "I Can Use a Tourist Visa and Switch Later"

Reality: L Visa holders who want to study in China may be required to leave and re-enter to obtain the correct visa status . This is expensive and operationally complicated.

Pitfall 5: "I'll Figure Out the Residence Permit Extension at the Airport"

Reality: Residence Permit renewals require processing time (5 working days). You cannot extend your stay at the airport upon departure. Overstay fines are assessed at the border.
