# Foreword

You have secured a job offer in China. Your employer has told you they will "handle the visa." Your friends have told you to "just get a work permit." Neither of these statements is sufficient preparation for what you are about to navigate.

This is not a generic overview of China's work culture. This is the step-by-step operational manual that covers: what documents you must obtain before you leave home, how to avoid having your residence permit rejected at the immigration desk, whether your employer is legally required to pay your social insurance, what happens if you are fired or want to quit, and how to enforce your legal rights when things go wrong.

This guide draws from official Chinese government regulations on work permits and residence permits, actual legal case outcomes from Chinese labor arbitration tribunals and courts across multiple provinces, and the current operational procedures of Beijing's work permit processing authorities. Every recommendation in this guide is grounded in enforceable legal requirements, not hearsay.


# Part I: The Legal Framework – What You Are Entering


## 1.1 The Three-Document System

Working legally in China requires three separate but interdependent documents:


| Document | Issued By | Purpose | Validity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Work Permit (外国人工作许可证) | Ministry of Science and Technology / local bureaus | Authorizes you to work for a specific employer in a specific role | Tied to employment contract; up to 5 years for A-class talent |
| Work-Class Residence Permit (工作类居留许可) | Exit & Entry Administration (public security bureau) | Authorizes your physical stay in China for work purposes | Up to 5 years; must be renewed before expiry |
| Z-Visa (Z字签证) | Chinese embassy/consulate abroad | Entry permit to travel to China and activate the above | Single-entry; valid 30 days after arrival |

Critical rule: You cannot work in China with only a Z-visa. The Z-visa is a transit document. Within 30 days of arrival, you must apply for the Work-Class Residence Permit at the local Exit & Entry Administration. Failure to do so results in overstay fines (RMB 500 per day) and potential visa cancellation.


## 1.2 The Three Talent Categories (A, B, C)

China classifies foreign workers into three tiers, which determine processing speed, document requirements, and renewal terms:

Category A (High-End Talent): No age, education, or work experience restrictions. Certain document requirements may be replaced by a personal commitment letter (承诺制).

Category B (Professional Talent): Must be under Chinese statutory retirement age (60 for men, 55 for women). Requires degree, work experience (2+ years typically), and complete documentation.

Category C (Other/Unskilled): Strict quotas; rarely issued to new foreign workers outside specific sectors (e.g., performing arts, short-term technical assignments).

Your employer determines your category based on salary, qualifications, and the role. If your salary is below RMB 400,000 annually, you are almost certainly Category B and must provide full documentation.


# Part II: Pre-Departure – What You Must Prepare Before Leaving Home


## 2.1 Document Authentication – The New Apostille Rule (Critical)

As of November 7, 2023, China acceded to the Hague Apostille Convention. This dramatically simplified document legalization.

Before November 2023: You needed your documents notarized, then authenticated by your country's foreign ministry, then legalized by the Chinese embassy/consulate — a process taking 4 weeks.

From November 2023 onward: You only need:

Your original document (degree, police clearance, etc.)

Apostille from the competent authority in your country (e.g., U.S. Department of State, UK Foreign Office)

A certified Chinese translation

This cuts processing time to 1 weeks.


## 2.2 Documents You Must Obtain Before Boarding the Plane

Based on the official work permit application requirements for Beijing, you need the following original authenticated documents before you depart:


| Document | Requirement Details | Time to Obtain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Highest Degree Certificate | Must be Apostilled. If issued outside China, must have Apostille from the issuing country's competent authority | 1 weeks with Apostille |
| Police Clearance Certificate | Must be issued within 6 months of application. Must show no criminal record. Must be Apostilled. Warning: Sworn/declaratory statements of no criminal record are NOT accepted | 2 weeks (country dependent) |
| Medical Examination Report | Must be issued by a Chinese-designated medical institution after arrival, OR a physical examination record verified by Chinese health authorities | Obtain after arrival (see Section 3.3) |
| Employment Contract | Must be in Chinese. Must include: work location, job description, compensation, start date. Must be signed by you and stamped by employer | Negotiate before departure |
| Work Experience Verification | Letters from previous employers detailing position, duration, and projects. Must include contact details for verification | 2 weeks |

Critical note on police clearance: If you have lived outside your home country for more than one continuous year, you must obtain a police clearance from that country as well. "Frequent traveler" status does not exempt you.


## 2.3 The Z-Visa Application

Before your embassy appointment, your employer must obtain Work Permit Notification (工作许可通知) online through the Chinese work permit system. This electronic document is the basis for your Z-visa application.

Documents for the Z-visa:

Work Permit Notification (printed)

Valid passport (minimum 6 months validity)

Visa application form

Passport-size photos (Chinese specifications — usually 33mm x 48mm)

Invitation letter from employer

Timeline: Work permit notification takes 5 working days from submission. Visa processing takes 3 working days depending on your country.


# Part III: Arrival – The First 30 Days (Critical Window)

The first 30 days after arrival are the most operationally intense period of your move. Missing deadlines during this window creates cascading legal and practical problems.


## 3.1 Day 1: Accommodation Registration (24-Hour Rule)

Legal requirement: All foreign nationals must register their accommodation within 24 hours of arrival.

Hotel: Reception scans your passport. Registration is automatic. Keep the registration slip.

Private rental / Airbnb / friend's apartment: You must register at the local police station (派出所) in person.

What to bring to the police station:

Original passport

Lease agreement (or written invitation letter from host)

Host's Chinese ID copy (房东身份证复印件)

Property ownership certificate copy (房产证复印件) — the host must provide this

If you are staying with a Chinese citizen who owns the property: They must accompany you or provide all documents above. Police stations may also require the host to sign a "statement of responsibility" (治安责任书) confirming they are responsible for you during your stay.

Failure to register triggers a fine of RMB 500,000 and may complicate your residence permit application later.


## 3.2 Day 1: Physical Examination at Designated Hospital

You must undergo a health examination at a hospital designated by China Customs (海关). In most major cities, this is the International Travel Health Care Center (国际旅行卫生保健中心).

What to bring:

Original passport

Original physical exam form from your home country (if any)

2 passport photos

Fasting (for blood tests)

What they test:

General physical examination

Chest X-ray (for tuberculosis screening)

Blood tests (HIV, syphilis, hepatitis)

ECG

Timeline: Results take 3 working days. You receive a Health Certificate (健康证明书) — valid for 6 months — which you must submit for your residence permit.

Cost: Approximately RMB 500 depending on the city. Your employer may reimburse this.


## 3.3 Day 5: Work Permit Application (If Not Already Processed)

If your employer did not process the work permit before your arrival (some employers do this after entry), you must apply now.

The process (simplified):

Employer creates an account on the Foreigners Work Management System

Employer uploads all documents:

Your authenticated degree (with Apostille + Chinese translation)

Your authenticated police clearance (with Apostille + Chinese translation)

Your employment contract

Your health certificate

Your passport information page

Digital photo

Approval: 5 working days. You receive an electronic Work Permit Card (or notification).

Physical card: Some cities now issue physical cards; others issue only electronic records. Beijing issues a physical card with a QR code.

Crucial: The work permit is tied to your specific employer and specific job role. If you change employers, you cannot simply "transfer" — you must apply for a new work permit with the new employer, and the process restarts.
