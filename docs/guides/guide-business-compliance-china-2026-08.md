# Part I: Corporate Governance — The Company Law Update


## 1.1 The 5-Year Paid-in Capital Rule

Article 47 of the 2024 Company Law, effective July 1, 2024, introduced a critical change: the registered capital of a limited liability company must be fully paid in within 5 years from the establishment date.

Before 2024: Registered capital could be declared as "subscribed" with no fixed payment timeline. This led to WFOEs declaring RMB 10 million in registered capital with only RMB 100,000 actually paid.

From 2024 onward: All capital committed at registration must be physically transferred to the WFOE's bank account within 5 years.

Practical implications:

Do not over-declare registered capital. Declare only what you realistically need for operations and what you can fund within 5 years.

Failure to meet the payment deadline triggers administrative penalties and potential deregistration.

Existing WFOEs have a transition period. Check whether your previously declared capital needs to be adjusted downward or paid in earlier.

Worked example: You declare RMB 1,000,000 registered capital. You must transfer the full RMB 1,000,000 (or the foreign currency equivalent) to the WFOE's capital account within 5 years. If you only transfer RMB 500,000 by year 5, you are in violation.


## 1.2 The Legal Representative — Not a Figurehead

The Company Law requires every company to have a legal representative. This individual is personally liable for certain company obligations.

Who can be legal representative:

Director or Manager (General Manager) of the company

Why this matters:

The legal representative is the primary contact for regulatory authorities

Criminal liability for certain violations can attach to the legal representative personally

Legal representative changes require SAMR approval and re-issuance of the company chop

Practical recommendation: Do not use a local agent's nominee as your legal representative. Designate a real, accountable executive — ideally from the parent company.


# Part II: Tax Compliance — Your Actual Obligations


## 2.1 Corporate Income Tax (CIT) — The 25% Standard

China's standard Corporate Income Tax rate is 25%. However, effective rates vary significantly:


| Situation | Effective Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Standard WFOE | 25% |
| High & New Technology Enterprise (HNTE) | 15% |
| Small Low-Profit Enterprise (SME threshold) | ~5% |
| Western Region/Encouraged Industry | 15% |
| Qianhai (Shenzhen), Hainan FTP, Lingang (Shanghai) | 15% |

HNTE requirements:

Core technology must be in a national encouraged field

R&D expenses ≥ 3% of revenue

Technology-based income ≥ 60% of total revenue

University-level employees ≥ 30% of workforce

Critical point: HNTE qualification is determined by what you actually do and where you are registered. A consulting WFOE with minimal R&D will not qualify.


## 2.2 Quarterly Filing Schedule


| Period | Filing Deadline | What You File |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | April 15 | Quarterly CIT prepayment |
| Q2 | July 15 | Quarterly CIT prepayment |
| Q3 | October 15 | Quarterly CIT prepayment |
| Q4 + Annual | January 15 (Q4), May 31 (Annual) | Annual CIT return + audit report |

Failure to file on time triggers late filing penalties and interest on unpaid tax.


## 2.3 Value-Added Tax (VAT) — The Service vs. Goods Distinction


| Transaction Type | VAT Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Goods (13%) | Standard rate |
| Most Services (6%) | Standard rate |
| Transportation/Construction (9%) | Standard rate |

What matters for foreign companies:

Imported services (e.g., software subscription from overseas parent) may be subject to withholding VAT in China

Service agreements with overseas parties must clearly specify who bears the VAT liability

Proper Fapiao (发票) management is non-negotiable — without compliant invoices, you cannot claim input VAT credits


## 2.4 Withholding Tax on Dividends

When your WFOE distributes profits to its foreign parent, a 10% withholding tax applies.

Reduced rates under tax treaties:

Hong Kong holding company: 5% (with proper treaty benefits claim)

Singapore: 5% (with proper documentation)

United States: 10% (standard)

United Kingdom: 10% (standard)

Paperwork required: To claim treaty benefits, you must file a Treaty Benefits Application (usually Form 501) with the local tax bureau, demonstrating that the recipient is the beneficial owner.


## 2.5 Individual Income Tax (IIT) — Employer Withholding Obligation

Your WFOE is legally required to withhold IIT on all employee salaries and remit to the tax bureau monthly.

2026 rates (progressive):


| Annual Taxable Income (RMB) | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 36,000 | 3% |
| 36,001,000 | 10% |
| 144,001,000 | 20% |
| 300,001,000 | 25% |
| 420,001,000 | 30% |
| 660,001,000 | 35% |
| Over 960,000 | 45% |

What your WFOE must do:

Register all employees with the tax bureau

Calculate tax on monthly payroll

Remit withheld tax by the 15th of the following month

File annual reconciliation for each employee between March-June


# Part III: Social Insurance and Housing Fund — Mandatory Employer Costs


## 3.1 "Social Insurance Entering the Tax System" (社保入税)

As of 2026, social insurance contributions are administered by the tax bureau, not the social insurance bureau. This means:

Contribution bases must now match individual income tax declaration bases

Discrepancies between declared salary for IIT and declared salary for social insurance trigger automatic alerts

Non-compliance is now easier to detect


## 3.2 Employer Contribution Rates (Tier Cities)


| Component | Employee % | Employer % |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pension | 8% | 16% |
| Medical | 2% | ~9% |
| Unemployment | 0.5% | 0.5% |
| Work Injury | - | 0.2.9% |
| Maternity | - | ~0.5% |
| Housing Fund | 5% | 5% |
| TOTAL EMPLOYER COST |  | ~35% of gross salary |

Example: A gross salary of RMB 30,000/month in Shanghai requires the employer to pay approximately RMB 10,500,500 in social insurance and housing fund contributions on top of the gross salary.


## 3.3 Foreign Employees — Obligations and Exemptions

Foreign employees are generally subject to the same social insurance obligations as Chinese employees, except where bilateral social security agreements apply (e.g., Germany, South Korea).

Bilateral agreement impact: If your employee is from a country with a social security agreement with China, they may be exempt from certain contributions (typically pension) for a limited period.

Housing fund for foreigners: Foreign employees are entitled to participate in the housing fund. Upon leaving China, they can withdraw the full balance (employee + employer contributions).


# Part IV: Data Compliance — PIPL, DSL, and CSL


## 4.1 The Three-Law Framework


| Law | Key Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) | How personal data is collected, processed, stored, and transferred |
| Data Security Law (DSL) | Classification and protection of "important data" |
| Cybersecurity Law (CSL) | Network security, IT systems, and storage compliance |

These laws are not optional. They apply to all foreign enterprises operating in China that handle personal data or operate IT systems in China.


## 4.2 Cross-Border Data Transfer — The Exporter's Burden

Transferring personal data from China to overseas is heavily restricted.

Three mechanisms for lawful transfer:


| Mechanism | Applicability | Process |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Security Assessment | Transfer of "important data" or large-scale personal data (1M+ individuals) | Government review and approval |
| Standard Contract | Smaller-scale personal data transfer | File with local cyberspace authority |
| Certification | Under certain circumstances | Obtain certification from accredited entity |

What qualifies as personal data: Names, passport numbers, contact details, health information, salary records, performance data — essentially any data that can identify a natural person.

Practical implication: Your WFOE cannot automatically sync employee data to the parent company's global HR system without satisfying these requirements.


## 4.3 Sensitive Personal Data — Higher Scrutiny

Sensitive personal data includes:

Biometric data (fingerprints, facial recognition)

Health information

Financial information

Location data

Requirements:

Explicit, separate consent from the individual

Justification for why this data must be transferred overseas

Data protection impact assessment (DPIA) for sensitive data transfers


# Part V: Anti-Corruption and Compliance


## 5.1 Anti-Unfair Competition Law — Gifts and Entertainment

The Anti-Unfair Competition Law prohibits bribery and improper business practices.

What is prohibited:

Providing cash or cash-equivalent gifts to government officials or business partners

Entertainment that is excessive or "expectation-setting"

Facilitation payments (even small ones)

What is generally allowed:

Reasonable business meals (with documentation)

Modest corporate gifts (company-branded items, not cash)

Legitimate business entertainment (within reasonable limits)

Documentation requirement: All business entertainment and gift expenses must be properly recorded with receipts and business purpose. Without documentation, they are treated as potential bribery.


## 5.2 Internal Compliance Measures

Best practices for foreign WFOEs:

Code of Conduct — Written policy on anti-corruption, gifts, and business ethics

Employee handbook provisions — Explicit prohibition of bribery

Regular training — Annual compliance training for all employees

Reporting channels — Established mechanism for employees to report concerns

Continuous monitoring — Regular audit of business transactions


# Part VI: Customs and Import/Export Compliance


## 6.1 Customs Registration

If your WFOE engages in import or export activities, you must:

Register with Customs (海关) — obtain Customs Registration Number

Classify your products using the correct HS code (Harmonized System)

Declare all shipments accurately — incorrect HS codes trigger penalties and potential seizure


## 6.2 Key Compliance Requirements


| Requirement | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Proper HS code | Incorrect classification leads to wrong duty calculation and penalties |
| Complete documentation | Commercial invoices, certificates of origin, packing lists |
| Inspection/quarantine | Certain goods require additional approval |
| License requirements | Check whether your product requires import/export license |


# Part VII: ESG and Sustainability Reporting


## 7.1 Mandatory ESG Reporting

Starting for the business year 2024, listed companies in China are subject to mandatory ESG reporting requirements. A nationwide ESG reporting system is expected to be rolled out for all companies by 2030.

For foreign WFOEs:

Not currently mandatory for non-listed WFOEs

However, your parent company's ESG reporting obligations may require data from your China operations

Environmental compliance — emission standards, water/soil/air protection, waste disposal — remains strictly enforced regardless of reporting status


## 7.2 Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)

For manufacturing or construction projects: You must complete an Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) before proceeding. Violations can result in project delays or suspension.


# Part VIII: Annual Reporting Obligations


## 8.1 The Annual Business Report

All companies must file an annual business report with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Key information includes:

Basic company information

Employee count

Revenue and tax data

Shareholder information

Deadline: Typically June 30 each year.


## 8.2 Tax Filing Deadlines — Summary


| Filing | Deadline |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly VAT | 15th of following month |
| Monthly IIT Withholding | 15th of following month |
| Quarterly CIT | 15th of month following quarter end |
| Annual CIT Return | May 31 |
| Annual Business Report | June 30 |


# Part IX: Common Pitfalls — Real Cases

Pitfall 1: "I Can Use a Nominee Legal Representative"

Reality: Foreign founders sometimes default to a local agent's nominee for the legal representative role. This works until it doesn't. When compliance issues arise, the nominee has legal authority and can make decisions that bind the company — decisions you may not control.

Fix: Designate a real, accountable legal representative — ideally an executive of the parent company.

Pitfall 2: "I'll Optimize My Tax Structure After Setup"

Reality: WFOE structure affects HNTE qualification, Hainan FTP eligibility, the encouraged industries catalogue, and city-level subsidies. Set up first and optimise later, and you're paying 25% CIT when you could have been paying 15%.

Fix: Plan your structure — including city selection and business scope — before incorporation.

Pitfall 3: "I Don't Need an ICP Filing"

Reality: A WFOE without an ICP filing can't operate a Chinese-hosted website, can't run Baidu paid ads, can't integrate WeChat Pay.

Fix: Plan ICP at the same time as WFOE setup, not after.

Pitfall 4: "My Social Insurance Contributions Are Optional"

Reality: Social insurance is mandatory. "Measured hiring" in 2026 means foreign companies are now building more disciplined workforce planning processes. Non-compliance is increasingly detected due to tax bureau data integration.


# Part X: The WFOE Closure Process — An Exit Plan You Need


## 10.1 Why You Need to Know This

Even if you don't plan to close your WFOE, understanding the closure process informs your operations. Closing a WFOE takes 6 months — longer than opening one.


## 10.2 The Closure Sequence

Board resolution to dissolve and appoint liquidation committee

Public notice of dissolution (45-day creditor notice period)

Tax closing audit — typically 4 months. The tax bureau examines every year of operations

Customs deregistration (for trading WFOEs)

Employee settlement — severance, social insurance, housing fund close-out

Key insight: The tax closing audit is the longest step. If your WFOE has had tax issues, the audit will find them. Clean tax records significantly accelerate closure.
